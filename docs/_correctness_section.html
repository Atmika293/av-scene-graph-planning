Where the reference boxes come from. nuScenes has no separately hand-labelled 2D boxes. Its devkit derives them from the 3D annotations with export_2d_annotations_as_json.py, which projects each 3D box into every camera that sees it and clips the result to the image. The reference here comes from a fork of that script (scripts/validation/export_2d_annotations_debug.py): its projection logic is copied verbatim, so its final boxes are identical to the devkit's, but it also records the box corners after every intermediate transform stage, so a disagreement can be traced to the exact stage that caused it. A high IOU therefore means this project's own implementation of the projection chain agrees with the devkit's; it is not a comparison against independently labelled 2D boxes.

Every chart below is built on IOU (intersection-over-union) between our computed 2D bbox and the nuScenes reference bbox: \[\text{IOU} = \frac{|\text{Our box} \cap \text{Reference box}|}{|\text{Our box} \cup \text{Reference box}|}\] 1 means the two boxes match exactly; 0 means they don't overlap at all. Area (used on the x-axis below) is computed the same way for a single clipped box: \[\text{Area} = \max\big(0,\ (x_{max}-x_{min}) \cdot (y_{max}-y_{min})\big)\]

IOU vs. projected bbox area (full range)

Every annotation/camera pair compared, full IOU range. Each point is one pair; x-axis is our computed bbox's area in pixels (log scale, since areas span single-digit slivers to frame-filling boxes). On this dataset every point already sits within \([0.9997, 1.0000]\) of IOU regardless of area (see the zoomed chart below for the real spread), but low-area points still correlate with the lower end of that narrow band: IOU is more sensitive to a fixed absolute coordinate difference once a box's area is small, so low-area points are partly a measurement-sensitivity artifact, not purely evidence of a worse projection. That sensitivity is why visualize_projections.py's worst/best image selection excludes boxes below a minimum area.

IOU distribution by projected bbox area (binned)

Same data as the chart above, but grouped into 8 log-spaced bbox-area bins and zoomed to the data's actual IOU range (\([0.999655, 1.000000]\), not a guessed window). A zoomed *scatter* of this data would just be a dense horizontal smear: every pair's IOU differs from every other's by at most a few thousandths, and there are ~17.7k of them. Binning is what makes the small-vs-large pattern legible: a visibly lower median or wider spread in the small-area bins (left) than the large-area ones (right) is the area-sensitivity effect described above; a flat median across bins would mean area isn't actually driving the differences.

IOU distribution by category

IOU distribution per nuScenes category (every pair in that category, not just the 10 in the gallery below), zoomed to the same data-driven range. A category with a visibly lower median or wider spread than the rest would suggest the pipeline handles that category's typical size, shape, or occlusion pattern less well than others; points outside the visible range are still included in the box/median statistics, only clipped from view.

IOU distribution by camera channel

IOU distribution per camera channel. Six cameras share the same intrinsics/extrinsics pipeline code, so a channel with a systematically lower median or wider spread than the others would point at a channel-specific issue (e.g. a calibration or distortion-handling bug specific to that camera) rather than something wrong with the shared projection logic itself.
