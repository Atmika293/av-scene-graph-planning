Lift-Splat-Shoot

Philion & Fidler · ECCV 2020 · Reading · read for Phase 2

How to build a bird's-eye view from cameras alone, where depth has to be predicted rather than measured. My Phase 2 BEV does the same job geometrically, with LiDAR supplying every point's position, so most of my notes compare the two.

Summary

  • Problem: turn the images from a car's cameras into one top-down map (for example, which cells are vehicle or drivable road), for any number of cameras with known calibration.
  • Lift: each pixel is lifted into a frustum of points along its ray, one per discrete depth bin, weighted by a predicted depth distribution. Where my pipeline gets depth exactly from LiDAR, LSS has to predict it, and hedges across depths when unsure.
  • Splat: the lifted features from all six cameras are sum-pooled into a shared BEV grid of pillars: the learned counterpart of my raster's binning step.
  • Shoot: planning by scoring a set of template trajectories against a cost map predicted in BEV. Relevant later, in Phase 3's trajectory evaluation.
  • Training: end to end from BEV labels only. Depth is never supervised directly; the depth distribution is whatever makes the BEV output right.

My notes

  • Lift and splat aren't networks, and the CNN never sees the calibration. The only learned part is the per-image CNN that predicts a depth distribution α and a feature c per pixel from the image alone. Calibration enters in one fixed geometric step, pego = R · K⁻¹ · [u·d, v·d, d]ᵀ + t, after undoing the image resize and crop. That is my Phase 2 projection run backwards: pixel plus a depth guess to ego, instead of ego to pixel.
  • Splat sums everything in a cell instead of choosing one value. My raster keeps one value per cell (nearest, first camera, last drawn). LSS sums every feature that lands in a cell, from different depth bins, pixels and cameras, with no z-buffer. Placement is soft, because each pixel contributes to every bin along its ray, weighted by α. My own reasoning, not the paper's: near cells collect more rays than far ones.
  • Splat borrows the pillar from PointPillars. A pillar is a grid cell in x and y with no binning in z.
    • Every point in the footprint belongs to the same cell regardless of height.
    • z survives as a point feature, encoded by a PointNet and a max over the pillar. That avoids 3D convolutions, at the cost of squeezing vertical structure into one vector.
    • It is my raster idea with learned channels.
  • LSS resembles NeRF but runs the other way. Both discretize rays and weight the samples; the table below compares the rest.
